01 · The seed is the whole input

Your text is hashed (xmur3) into a 32-bit integer, which seeds a mulberry32 generator. Every ridge phase, dab colour, clump centre and walker position comes out of that single stream — nothing is drawn from Math.random().

Type melbourne, then change one letter. Whole new range. Type the original back and you get the identical image, byte for byte — so a 3× export matches the preview you approved.

Canvas 2D · Path2D · xmur3 + mulberry32 — every current browser

02 · Ridgelines from summed sines

Each layer is a 1-D profile built from four sine octaves with seeded frequency, amplitude and phase, plus one broad gaussian massif. Roughness scales the octave amplitudes, so 0 gives soft dunes and 100 gives a broken highland.

Push Ridge layers to 8 and Roughness to 90 — the sky shrinks to a slot and the hills stack into a collaged horizon.

Pure Canvas 2D paths — no GPU, no WebGL

03 · Pointillist fill, batched by colour

Each layer fills its polygon with thousands of dabs sampled by rejection, plus a handful of lighter clumps that read as rock and snow. Dabs are grouped into one Path2D per colour and filled in a single pass — 20k dabs cost a handful of draw calls.

Drop Dab size to 5 and raise Particle density to 100 for a dense stipple; go the other way for confetti.

Dab geometry is normalised, so density looks identical at 1× and 3×

04 · Export and share

Every control writes into the URL hash. Copy the link and the recipient opens the exact same landscape — seed, palette, terrain, frame and all. Save PNG (or press S) re-renders at the chosen scale off-screen so the export is clean at 3840px.

Use it as a hero or OG image: type the client's name, keep the palette on their brand, export, and you can regenerate the identical art next month at a different crop.
