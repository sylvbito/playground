Gamut Ramp Lab

A brand colour is a single point. A ramp is that point's hue held while lightness sweeps from a pale stop to a dark one — and the pale and dark ends are exactly where sRGB runs out. This tool builds the ramp, shows where the display's edge is, and measures what each way of resolving the overflow actually costs.

01 The ramp

What it is. The brand colour converted to OKLCH (lightness 0–1, chroma 0–0.4, hue in degrees), then spread across N stops with the hue held fixed and lightness swept. Chroma is the variable everybody argues about, and it is the one that breaks ramps: hold it flat and the near-white and near-black stops sit outside anything sRGB can print.

What to try. Start with #e6318e and drag Chroma ×. At ×0.6 the ramp is almost polite; at ×1.4 every one of the 16 stops is outside sRGB and panel 02 changes its verdict. Then switch the target to Display P3 and watch stops come back inside.

Support. oklch() — Chrome 111+, Safari 15.4+, Firefox 113+.

02 What clipping costs

What it is. Three ways to resolve a stop that will not fit. Naïve clip clamps each channel in the destination space (one line of code, and the origin of most "the ramp turned a different colour halfway down" bugs). Clamp chroma binary-searches chroma down until the colour fits, keeping lightness and hue exactly. CSS Color 4 is the spec algorithm: the same search with a ΔEok ≈ 0.02 tolerance, so it keeps more chroma than a strict fit while staying within one just-noticeable difference.

What to try. Read the three columns for a blue — #3898ec at Chroma ×1.4 clips every stop, bends hue by tens of degrees and slides lightness by a fifth of the scale, which is what makes the pale end of a clipped ramp look like a different colour rather than a lighter one. Then note that clipping is not always the worst by raw distance: it is the hue and lightness damage that shows.

Support. ΔEok with a 0.02 JND is the threshold CSS uses; color-mix() and oklch() both gamut-map with this algorithm in current Chrome and Safari.

03 Where the gamut ends

What it is. A gamut is not a box in OKLCH — it is a lobed shape. This is the slice through it at the ramp's hue: for every lightness, the most chromatic colour each display can actually show. Inside the sRGB fill is safe everywhere; the bands beyond it need P3 or Rec.2020 panels.

What to try. Paste a yellow-green and watch the sRGB boundary collapse — the green primary reaches its chroma peak at a lower lightness, so a flat-chroma ramp shoots straight out of the shape. The pale end is the trap: at L 0.95 sRGB holds between 0.024 and 0.107 chroma depending on hue (0.028 for this pink, 0.107 for the yellow-greens), against the 0.2+ a saturated brand colour carries — so a light tint built from a saturated brand colour is outside the gamut before you touch it.

Support. color(display-p3 …) — Chrome 111+, Safari 10.1+, Firefox 113+; color(rec2020 …) — Chrome 111+. Canvas readback (getImageData) is 8-bit sRGB.

04 The three ramps, and what to ship

What it is. The same intended ramp resolved by each method, side by side, with the CSS to take away. Targets other than sRGB also get a @supports ladder so an sRGB-only screen gets a usable fallback instead of a mangled colour.

What to try. Run the intended strip against the three resolved strips and look for the pale stops: clipped ones lose their relationship to the brand hue, chroma-clamped ones keep the hue and read as greyer siblings, CSS-mapped ones keep the most colour of the three. Then press S to save the stylesheet.

Support. Custom properties + @supports — every browser shipping oklch(); the fallback value is a plain hex, so nothing dark-launches.

05 Oracles — what the browser actually paints

    

      
What it is. A colour tool is only worth what it can be checked against, so this panel does the
        checking in the open. The canvas oracle paints each out-of-gamut oklch() string into a 1×1
        canvas and reads the rendered bytes back with getImageData, then compares them with this
        page's naïve-clip and CSS-mapping results. The culori button loads
        culori from a CDN on demand — an independent implementation of the
        same spec algorithm — and diffs its sRGB output against this page's.

      
What to try. Run the canvas oracle first, then culori. The result to expect on current Chrome is
        the finding this whole tool is built around: painting an out-of-gamut oklch() gets you the
        clipped bytes, not the spec's gamut-mapped ones — the two differ by tens of 255ths. Declaring a
        colour in a wide gamut does not get you gamut mapping; you have to compute it and ship in-gamut values,
        which is what panel 04 does. (The same check against a real CSS background, screenshotted and sampled
        during the build, gave byte-identical clipped output.)

      
Support. Canvas accepts CSS Color 4 syntax in Chrome 111+, Safari 16.4+, Firefox 113+; a browser
        that cannot parse the string is reported as unsupported rather than silently passed. The reverse OKLab
        matrices here are computed as exact inverses of Ottosson's forward matrices rather than copied from the
        paper's rounded table, which otherwise costs ≈1e-7 over a round trip.
